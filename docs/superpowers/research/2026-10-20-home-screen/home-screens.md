## Patterns most apps agree on

1. **Overdue sits above today and is never silently dropped.** Reminders, Todoist and TickTick all put overdue first or into Today (S). Todoist and TickTick add a bulk "Reschedule" or "Plan Your Day" pass (S).
2. **Events and to-dos live on the same screen, but events are visually set apart.** Things puts events at the top, Todoist groups them in an "event stack", and Reminders and Calendar use checkboxes to mark tasks (S). TickTick is the exception: it interleaves everything by time (S).
3. **Per-calendar inclusion is the main anti-swamp control.** Things, Todoist, Fantastical and Apple Calendar all let you choose which calendars feed the view (S). Fantastical goes further and switches calendar sets by Focus mode (S).
4. **Recurring items show only the next occurrence in list views.** Todoist does this for recurring tasks (S). No app I found does this for recurring *calendar events*, so treat it as a gap (Sp).
5. **Upcoming has a near band day by day, then coarser buckets.** Things uses 7 single days, then weeks or months (S/2nd). Todoist uses a week strip plus a day list (S).
6. **Empty days are hidden in lists.** Fantastical does this by default (S). A Mozilla user request argues that a "No events" header still helps people see free days (connect.mozilla.org/t5/ideas/allow-showing-all-days-in-today-pane/idc-p/120246).
7. **Capture is one "+" tap away, with natural-language dates**: Todoist, TickTick, Fantastical, Amie (S). The default landing spot is an Inbox: Things, Structured (S).
8. **"Next up" design lives in widgets**: the next item, a countdown, a switch to the current event, and a count of what's left (BusyCal, Fantastical) (S).

## Where they differ, and why

- **One merged list vs separate bands.** TickTick merges by time, which suits a day you want to work through in order. Things and Todoist keep events in a separate band, because they are task-first apps and events are context for the tasks (Sp on the reasoning).
- **Untimed vs timed first.** Reminders originally put untimed items above timed ones. A user argued this hides the morning's timed items, and iOS 18 made the order configurable (S, 512pixels). So the "right" order varies by user.
- **Automatic vs curated Today.** Reminders, Todoist and TickTick fill Today automatically from dates. Things fills it from start dates; a deadline alone does not pull a to-do in (S, /2803579/). Sunsama fills Today only through a deliberate ritual (S). Automatic is low effort but can overflow; curated stays short but costs a daily step.
- **Timeline vs list.** Structured is timeline-first (S). The others are list-first. A timeline makes gaps and the current time visible. A list fits more items on a phone.
- **Start date vs deadline.** Things separates "when I'll start" from "when it's due" (S). Most of the other apps use a single due date. This matters for assignments: a single date cannot show when to begin a paper that is due Friday.

## 6 design options for plaenicke's home screen

**1. Things-style stacked bands.** From top to bottom: Overdue (red, with a "reschedule all" action), Today's events shown compactly with "+N more", Today's to-dos and dues, This Evening, then Next 7 days as day headers with dues only and events collapsed to a count. *Pros:* proven structure, and to-dos stay central. *Cons:* events become secondary, and a day full of lectures still takes up the band unless option 4 is also used. (**concrete**)

**2. TickTick-style single time-sorted agenda.** One list for today, with to-dos and events interleaved by time, untimed items in an "anytime" group, a now-line, and a relative time on the next item ("in 42 min"). Later days are grouped by date below. *Pros:* matches how a day is actually lived, and the next-up item is obvious. *Cons:* recurring lectures interleave with deadlines and push them down the list, which is the known pain. (**concrete**)

**3. "Next up" hero card plus a deadline list.** The top card shows the current event with a progress bar, or the next event with a countdown, in the style of BusyCal's Countdown widget. Below it, a deadlines list with days-left chips (today / tomorrow / 3d), sorted by due date, plus overdue items. Events beyond the hero card live only in the calendar tab. *Pros:* the shortest answer to "what do I have to do", and it removes event swamping entirely. *Cons:* hides the rest of the day's schedule; one tap away. (**concrete**)

**4. Recurring-series collapse, layered onto any option.** Detect ICS events that share a UID/RRULE or a summary+weekday pattern. On the home screen, show only today's instances and keep later ones out of lists ("CS101, next Thu 10:00"). Add per-feed inclusion toggles, as Things, Todoist and Fantastical do: Canvas feed → deadlines band, class feed → schedule only. *Pros:* fixes the root cause. *Cons:* the heuristic for feeds without RRULE (expanded instances) is **speculative**. It needs a check against the real class-schedule ICS.

**5. Start-vs-due model for assignments.** Give `due` items an optional "start by" date, in the style of Things (S). The home screen surfaces an assignment from its start date onward, with an escalating label: "due in 5d" in neutral, "due tomorrow" in amber, "overdue 2d" in red. Swipe right to mark done, with undo. *Pros:* fits Canvas deadlines, and work shows up before the panic point. *Cons:* adds a field that imported Canvas items lack, so it needs a default offset; picking that default is **speculative**.

**6. Capture-first home with a pinned input.** An always-visible input at the top or bottom of Home parses natural language ("essay due fri", "idea: …", "lunch w/ Sam 1pm tmr") into type plus date, using Todoist and Fantastical's prefix and NL conventions (S). Undated items land in an Inbox/Ideas band. Option 1 or 3 sits below the input. *Pros:* fastest capture, and ideas are never lost. *Cons:* the iOS keyboard covers half the screen when the input is focused, and parsing ambiguity needs visible chips you can tap to undo, as TickTick does (S). The parser is **concrete** only for a small grammar.

**Empty and light days (applies to all):** show "Nothing due today", then pull forward the next due item and the next event ("Next: Stats quiz Thu"), so the screen is never blank. This is in line with the Mozilla request (S) and BusyCal's "Current & Upcoming shows when you're free" (S); the pull-forward itself is **Sp**.
